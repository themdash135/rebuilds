General practice · Your call script

How a home sale generally works — and what to ask Rooted at each step.

This is how residential sales are generally handled in the trade. It is not a description of Rooted’s own process — their website doesn’t publish one, and we are not going to invent it. Read the left column for context, then use the right column as the script when you phone. Commission, fees and timelines are never published online by anyone; ask for them directly.

01

Pricing conversation

Listing agents generally open with a comparative market analysis — recent nearby sales, current competition, and what that suggests as a list price.

Ask Rooted: “What has sold near me in Grayson in the last 90 days, and what would you list mine at?”
02

The listing agreement

Sellers generally sign a written agreement that sets the term, the commission, and how it is split with a buyer’s agent. In most states this is negotiable.

Ask Rooted: “What is your commission, how long is the term, and how do I cancel if it isn’t working?”
03

Prep, photos, and going live

Before a listing goes live, agencies generally arrange photography and may advise on repairs, decluttering or staging. Who pays for what varies by agency.

Ask Rooted: “What do you cover — photos, staging, cleaning — and what is on me?”
04

Marketing and showings

Listings are generally entered into the local MLS and syndicated out to the big portals from there. Showing logistics differ from team to team.

Ask Rooted: “Where will my listing appear, and who personally runs the showings and open houses?”
05

Offers and negotiation

Offers arrive with a price, a financing type, a due-diligence window and requested concessions. Negotiation covers all of it, not just price.

Ask Rooted: “When an offer lands, how fast do you reach me, and who does the negotiating?”
06

Contract to closing

After a contract is signed come inspection, appraisal, and lender conditions. Georgia closings are generally handled by a closing attorney rather than an escrow company.

Ask Rooted: “Who is my point of contact between contract and closing, and what could still derail it?”

Buying instead? Ask these three.

  1. “How do I get on a showing this week for the addresses I found on your Home Search?”
  2. “What do I need in hand — pre-approval, proof of funds — before we write an offer?”
  3. “What does representing me cost me, and who pays it in this market?”
