General HVAC guidance — not this company’s policy

Repair or replace: how the trade generally frames it

The following is standard industry rule-of-thumb for residential heating and cooling equipment. It is background so you can hold your own in the conversation. Alex’s Services has published no pricing, policy, or recommendation, and none is implied here — confirm every line with whoever inspects your system.

How these factors are generally weighed across the HVAC trade. Not a quote from Alex’s Services.
FactorUsually points to repairUsually points to replace
System ageUnder roughly 10 years and holding refrigerant charge.Past roughly 15 years, where parts availability starts to bite.
Failed partCapacitor, contactor, fan motor, thermostat, control board.Compressor or a leaking evaporator or condenser coil.
Refrigerant typeCurrent refrigerant, still readily stocked.An older phased-out refrigerant that has become costly to top up.
Repair cost vs. newWell under a third of a replacement quote.Approaching half of a replacement quote, or the second big repair in two seasons.
Comfort symptomsOne room off, or a duct or airflow problem that is fixable on its own.Whole-house temperature swings plus rising runtime on an aging system.

General guidance only, stated in industry terms. Ask for the specific failed part and a written estimate before agreeing to anything — the questions in the next section are written for exactly that call.

Before you hang up

Six questions to ask on the call

Diagnostic fees, hourly rates, lead times, financing, license number and insurance status are not published on Alex’s site or their Google listing, so this page will not state them. Ask, and write the answers down.

  1. “What is the service-call or diagnostic fee, and does it come off the repair?”Settle the cost of showing up before anyone is dispatched.
  2. “When could someone actually get here?”Published hours are Mon–Fri 8 AM–5 PM and Saturday 8 AM–12 PM; the next open slot is a separate question.
  3. “Are you licensed and insured for this work — what is the license number?”Ask for it verbally and again on the invoice.
  4. “Will I get a written estimate before the repair starts?”The single best protection against a surprise total.
  5. “Which exact part failed, and what does it cost on its own?”Turns ‘you need a new system’ into a checkable claim.
  6. “What warranty covers the part and the labour?”Confirm the length of each, separately.
