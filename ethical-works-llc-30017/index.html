Washing 101 — general trade background

What the trade generally does, surface by surface

This is background on exterior cleaning in general, not a description of our methods — we do not publish our process anywhere, and this page will not put words in our mouth. Use it to ask us a sharper question when you call.

General industry practice for exterior cleaning. Confirm anything on this table with us before work starts — your surface, its age and its condition all change the answer.
SurfaceHow the trade generally treats itQuestion to ask us
Concrete driveway, walkway, patio Usually the surface most tolerant of high pressure; a surface cleaner attachment is commonly used to avoid the striping that a bare wand leaves. “Will this leave wand stripes, and do you rinse the run-off away from the lawn?”
Vinyl or painted siding Commonly cleaned by “soft washing” — low pressure plus a cleaning solution — because high pressure can drive water behind panels or strip paint. “Is this soft wash or high pressure on my siding, and what does that do to the paint?”
Asphalt shingle roof Shingle manufacturers generally warn against pressure washing; the standard approach is low or no pressure, since pressure dislodges the protective granules. “Do you clean roofs, and if so at what pressure?”
Wood deck or fence Softer than it looks — too much pressure raises and tears the grain, and the damage is permanent once it is done. “What pressure do you use on wood, and will it need re-sealing after?”
Plants and landscaping below the work Where cleaning solutions are used, the usual precaution is pre-wetting and rinsing the beds so nothing sits on the foliage. “How do you protect the shrubs under the wall you are washing?”
