How large-tree transplanting generally works

Native Tree has not published its own step-by-step process, so what follows is how spade transplanting is done across the trade — useful for knowing what to expect and what to ask. Confirm every specific with them on the call.

  1. 01

    Sizing and season

    In general the trunk diameter decides which spade can lift the tree, and the species plus the time of year decide whether it should be moved at all. Dormant-season digging is the trade's usual preference for deciduous stock. Ask them what your specific tree and month allow.

  2. 02

    Dig, lift, and match the hole

    Spade work typically cuts the receiving hole first, then returns to cut and lift the tree so the root ball spends the least possible time out of the ground. Larger or tighter jobs are generally craned instead, with the ball boxed or burlapped — both approaches appear in their photographs.

  3. 03

    Set, backfill, water in

    The tree is set to grade, backfilled and watered, and often staked. Across the trade, aftercare watering in the first seasons is what decides survival — ask who is responsible for it on your job, and what, if anything, is guaranteed.

The fastest way to find out if your tree is a candidate is to describe it out loud.

Before you hire anyone

Six questions for the phone call.

Native Tree does not publish prices, scheduling windows or insurance paperwork, and this page will not guess at them. Take these to the call — they are the same questions worth asking any tree contractor.

Ask thisWhy it matters
“Can you send a certificate of insurance, and do you carry workers' comp?”Tree work is the highest-risk residential trade. Coverage decides who pays for a dropped limb or an injured crew member.
“What does moving a tree this size cost, and what's included?”Establishes whether the quote covers digging, hauling, setting, backfill, staking and clean-up — or only some of them.
“Is my tree a candidate at this trunk size, species and season?”Not every tree survives a move. Better to hear no on the phone than after the spade arrives.
“How far out are you scheduling right now?”Transplant windows are seasonal and equipment is shared across jobs. Get the date before you plan around it.
“What survival guarantee, if any, comes with it — and what watering is on me?”Aftercare usually decides whether a transplant takes. Know the split before work starts.
“Do you have this species in stock at the size I want?”They sell mature stock as well as moving yours. Availability changes with the season.
