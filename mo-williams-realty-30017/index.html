Category background — how residential deals generally run

The shape of a transaction, start to finish.

General to residential real estate, not a description of this office's procedure. Every one of these steps has a “how does he do it” version — that is what the phone call is for.

The first conversation

What you are trying to do, by when, and what it needs to cost. Buyers and sellers both start here, and it is usually the shortest call of the whole process.

Numbers before decisions

Buyers generally line up financing so an offer is credible; sellers generally look at recent comparable sales before naming a price. Neither number should be guessed off the internet.

On the market, or out looking

Sellers: prep, photography, listing, showings. Buyers: touring, and moving quickly when the right one appears. This is the stretch where responsiveness matters most.

Offer and negotiation

Price is one term among many — closing date, contingencies, repairs and what conveys can matter as much as the headline number.

Under contract

Inspection, appraisal, and lender conditions run on deadlines. Missing one has consequences, which is why this stage is mostly calendar management.

Closing

Final walkthrough, signing, keys. Then the part nobody photographs: moving.

Ask these when you call

Commission, pricing, timelines and credentials are not published on this site or on the Google listing, so do not guess at them — get them from him, in his words, on the first call.

  1. What would you list my home at, and which comparable sales are you using?Ask to see the comps, not just the number.
  2. What is your commission, and exactly what does it cover?Photography, marketing, showings — get it itemised.
  3. What is your Georgia real estate licence number, and which brokerage do you write under?Both are verifiable independently.
  4. How long are homes like mine taking to go under contract right now?A local answer beats a national headline.
  5. Who handles showings, calls and offers — and how fast do you come back to me?Response time is the thing sellers complain about most.
  6. Do you represent buyers and sellers, and how do you handle it if both sides are yours?Fair question. Ask it early.
