  • Wet bottomland and saturated clay. The river headwaters rise in this area, and soaked ground is where uprooting risk comes from — the wind usually just finishes it.

Before you book anyone

What to ask on the phone

We don't publish prices, because no two trees price the same and a per-tree number on a website is a guess. Here's the short list of questions that gets you a real comparison between any two companies — us included.

  • "Can you send the certificate of insurance before the crew rolls?" Ours goes out on request, ahead of the work.
  • "Is the on-site look free, and who comes out?" Ours is free, and it's a walk of the property, not a driveway glance.
  • "Is the number written down before anyone starts?" Ask for a written scope that lists what's included.
  • "Is haul-off and cleanup in that number, or extra?" On our jobs it's in the scope: debris hauled, driveway blown off.
  • "How deep does the stump grind go?" Below grade is a different job from flush-cut. Say which one you want.
  • "What's the plan if a straight fell won't work?" In most yards it won't. The answer should be rigging or a crane pick, with a drop zone.

The five things everybody asks

Straight answers

Is this tree dangerous?

You get a real assessment first, not a removal quote by default. We look at the whole tree — lean, root flare, what's above it and what's under it — and around here saturated clay and graded root zones do a lot of the damage people blame on wind. If it can be saved, we say so.

Will it hit my house?

The drop zone is planned before a saw comes out, so nothing gets hit. Where a straight fell won't work — which is most yards — the tree comes down in sections on rigging, or gets picked with the crane.

What will the work cost?

No two trees price the same, so no price is published. The on-site look is free, and one phone call gets you a real answer and a written number. You see the written scope, and what's included in it, before anyone pulls a cord.

Will the crew damage my yard?

Debris hauled, driveway blown off, ruts avoided. On the rural properties around Loganville the long gravel drives and soft ground are the real risk, so equipment access gets planned as part of the job.

Will everything be cleaned up?

Full cleanup is in the scope on every job. The job ends when the yard is clean — no brush pile left behind.

Do you take the stump too?

The stump goes below grade if you want the space back. Say so when you call so it's written into the scope rather than added afterwards, and ask how deep the grind goes.
